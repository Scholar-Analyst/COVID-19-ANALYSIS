### Project Overview
This project explores COVID-19 data in Nigeria to understand the distribution of infections, recoveries, active cases and deaths across different states. The analysis focuses on identifying geographical variations, comparing state-level statistics and extracting public health insights using Microsoft Power BI.

### Geographical Distribution and State-Level Analysis
The geographical analysis revealed considerable differences in reported COVID-19 statistics across Nigerian states. 
- Confirmed Cases: Lagos recorded the highest number of confirmed infections, followed by the Federal Capital Territory and Rivers State.
- Deaths: Lagos also recorded the highest number of reported COVID-19 deaths among the locations represented.
- Discharged Cases: Lagos recorded the highest number of discharged cases, while Rivers, Kaduna, Plateau and Oyo also recorded notable figures.
- Active Cases: Lagos recorded the highest number of active cases, with variations observed across other states.
- 
Interpretation: These findings demonstrate that COVID-19 cases were not evenly distributed across Nigeria. Lagos consistently appeared among the locations with the highest reported figures across the indicators examined.

### Analysis of States with the Highest Confirmed COVID-19 Cases in Nigeria
The geographical analysis identified five states with the highest reported confirmed COVID-19 cases in the dataset. Lagos State recorded the highest number, with 101,683 confirmed cases, followed by the Federal Capital Territory (Abuja) with 28,851 cases, Rivers State ranked third with 16,960 cases, while Kaduna and Plateau recorded 11,315 and 10,259 cases, respectively.

### Analysis of States with the Lowest Confirmed COVID-19 Cases in Nigeria
The geographical analysis identified five states with the lowest reported confirmed COVID-19 cases in the dataset. Kogi State recorded the lowest number, with only 5 confirmed cases, followed by Zamfara (375), Kebbi (480), Yobe (634) and Jigawa (669).

### Key Public Health Insights
1. High Recovery Rate: The analysis recorded a discharge/recovery rate of approximately 96.71%, indicating that most reported COVID-19 cases in the dataset were discharged.
2. Low Reported Death Rate: The reported death rate was approximately 1.21% of confirmed cases. This represents the proportion of reported cases resulting in deaths in the dataset, not the risk of death for the general population.
3. Uneven Geographical Distribution: COVID-19 cases were not evenly distributed across Nigerian states. Lagos recorded the highest numbers of confirmed, active, discharged cases, and reported deaths.
4. Active Cases Require Attention: The analysis identified 5,378 active cases, representing approximately 2.08% of confirmed cases. This highlights the importance of continued monitoring, testing, treatment, and follow-up.
5. Importance of State-Level Surveillance: Differences in reported cases across states highlight the need for effective disease surveillance, timely reporting, and targeted public health interventions.
6. Data-Driven Healthcare Planning: The findings demonstrate how data analytics and Power BI can support public health decision-making by helping authorities monitor disease trends and identify areas requiring attention.

### Public health Implication
The findings highlight the importance of strengthening healthcare systems, improving disease surveillance, promoting public health education, maintaining effective testing and reporting systems, and ensuring that healthcare resources are distributed according to local needs.

Note: These insights are based on reported data downloaded from Kaggle. Differences between states may also reflect population size, testing coverage, and reporting practices. Therefore, the results should be interpreted as patterns in the available dataset rather than a complete measure of the pandemic’s impact.

### Conclusion
In conclusion, the analysis establishes that COVID-19 was an important public health challenge in Nigeria and that its distribution varied across geographical locations. It emphasises the need for sustained disease surveillance, improved healthcare preparedness, public health education and equitable access to healthcare services to minimise the impact of future infectious disease outbreaks.

### Public Health Recommendation
Based on the analysis, the following public health recommendations are proposed:

1. Strengthen Disease Surveillance: Improve COVID-19 monitoring, testing, and reporting across all Nigerian states.
2. Improve Healthcare Facilities: Provide adequate medical equipment, healthcare personnel, and treatment facilities, particularly in areas with high reported cases.
3. Promote Public Health Awareness: Encourage health education on vaccination, hygiene, and preventive measures.
4. Ensure Data-Driven Decisions: Use data analytics tools such as Power BI to monitor disease trends and support effective healthcare planning.
5. Improve Data Quality: Encourage accurate, consistent, and timely reporting of public health statistics.
6. Target High-Burden Areas: Prioritise public health interventions based on local disease trends while considering population size and testing coverage.

Overall Recommendation: Nigerian public health authorities should strengthen disease surveillance, healthcare infrastructure, public awareness, and data-driven decision-making to improve preparedness and response to future disease outbreaks.
